How it works

Drag to orbit, pinch or scroll to zoom, double-tap the view to stop following. Space bar drops.

Every object is a rigid body made of small flat panels plus spheres and point masses. Each panel feels its own local wind (including the body's spin), and from that gets lift, drag, stall, and a centre of pressure that walks from the quarter chord toward mid-chord as the angle of attack grows. Separated flow also gets the wake's tipping torque, scaled by the plate's added mass, which is what makes a card flutter or tumble instead of falling flat. Tufts show the local flow and turn red where it has separated. Gliders launch at the speed where their own lift balances weight; SM is the static margin, the distance from centre of mass to neutral point in wing chords. Air density 1.225 kg/m³.
